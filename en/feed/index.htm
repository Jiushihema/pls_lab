Physical Layer Secret Key Generation Assisted by Fluid Antenna Systems

Zhiyu Huang and Guyue Li and Hao Xu and Derrick Wing Kwan Ng

IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications

Abstract

Key Contributions

  • Two FAS-assisted PLKG models are proposed: one for scenarios where Eve's estimation is independent of legitimate users' estimation, and another for correlated scenarios considering spatial correlation between the eavesdropping channel and the legitimate channel; approximate SKR expressions are derived via mutual information and joint entropy analysis, explicitly incorporating the FAS port activation matrix and spatial correlation structure into the SKR analysis, and it is proven that the SKR gain increases with the largest eigenvalue of the spatial correlation matrix at the base station.
  • Algorithm design for solving non-convex optimization problems: non-convex terms are relaxed via SCA combined with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and a reweighted ℓ1\ell_1​ norm method is proposed to promote sparsity in port activation; further leveraging Rayleigh quotient theory and the oscillatory decay characteristics of Bessel-function-based spatial correlation, a low-complexity, near-optimal sliding-window port selection framework is proposed to approximate exhaustive search results, significantly reducing computational complexity.
  • Simulation validation and performance boundary revelation: the performance of FAS-assisted PLKG is revealed as an implementation boundary for achieving high SKR with minimal RF chains; results show that the scheme significantly outperforms fixed-antenna schemes in both independent and spatially correlated scenarios, and the sliding-window method consistently outperforms the reweighted ℓ1\ell_1norm method at the same complexity order while closely approximating the optimal selection of all ports via exhaustive search.

Method Overview

System and channel model: a MISO system employing the TDD protocol is considered, where a multi-antenna base station Alice is equipped with a linear fluid antenna surface containing $N$ RF chains, and $M$ preset ports share these RF chains and are uniformly distributed over a linear space of length $\lambda W$. A FAS-correlated channel model is adopted, where the elements of the spatial correlation matrix $J_A$ at Alice are characterized by the zero-order Bessel function of the first kind $J_0$, and ports can be adaptively selected via software control to exploit spatial correlation for enhancing the secret key rate.Beamforming and SKR derivation: Alice and Bob obtain channel state information through reciprocal channel probing combined with least-squares estimation, and Alice then constructs an equivalent channel aligned with Bob's scalar estimate via beamforming. The secret key rate $R_{SK}$ is defined as the conditional mutual information of the legitimate parties' channel estimates, $R_{SK}=I(\hat{h}_a;\hat{h}_b\mid \hat{h}^d_e,\hat{h}^u_e)$, and approximate expressions are derived for both i.i.d. and spatially correlated eavesdropping scenarios, with the core quantity being $w^H J_A w$.Joint optimization and port selection: the SKR is maximized under the transmit power constraint $|w|^2_2\le P_A$ and the sparsity constraint $|w|_0\le N$. Since the objective increases monotonically with $\beta_{ab}w^H J_A w$, the problem reduces to maximizing $w^H J_A w$, which is successively convexified via eigendecomposition and SCA combined with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, with a reweighted $\ell_1$ norm promoting sparsity. Furthermore, based on Rayleigh quotient theory and the oscillatory decay characteristics of Bessel functions, a sliding-window port selection method is proposed: a window of length $N$ slides over the $M$ ports, selecting the consecutive port combination maximizing $w^H J_A w$ as the initial vector before power optimization, reducing complexity from exponential to polynomial order $O((M+N-1)M^2)$.

Experimental Results

FAS-assisted PLKG significantly outperforms fixed-antenna schemes in both channel environments and achieves higher secret key rates with fewer RF chains: as shown in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4, the SKR increases monotonically with the power budget $P_A$ and gradually saturates; the FAS scheme achieves or even exceeds the performance of fixed-antenna schemes with more activated antennas (”FA Opt, $N=7$”) using fewer activated antennas (”FAS Opt, $N=5$”). Regarding the impact of the number of RF chains (Fig. 8, Fig. 9), the SKR improves monotonically as $N$ increases, with the spatially correlated scenario showing a 38.2% SKR increase while the i.i.d. scenario increases only 4.2%, indicating significantly higher sensitivity to the number of RF chains in the former. Additionally, the sliding-window method (”FAS Sliding Window”) consistently outperforms the reweighted $\ell_1$ norm method (”FAS Opt”) in terms of convergence, key disagreement rate (KDR, Fig. 7), and the impact of normalized size $W$ (Fig. 12, Fig. 13), while closely approximating exhaustive search (”FAS Traverse”); in robustness tests (Fig. 16, Fig. 17), the FAS scheme degrades more slowly as the spatial correlation matrix estimation error $\sigma^2_\Delta$ increases. In terms of convergence, approximately 15 iterations are required for $M=32$ and about 10 for $M=16$.

MirageBeam: Covert Adversarial Beamforming Feedback for High-Robust WiFi Sensing Hijacking

Zhiming Chu and Guyue Li and Tianxiang Xie and Dan Wu and Daqing Zhang

IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing

Abstract

Key Contributions

  • Proposes MirageBeam, the first adversarial framework against BFI-based sensing: Demonstrates that forged BFI frames can trigger untargeted and targeted misclassification in commercial WiFi human activity recognition systems.
  • Constructs a joint optimization problem: Jointly selects forged BFI amplitude and injection timing to maximize misclassification while enforcing statistical indistinguishability from normal activity sequences.
  • Extensive evaluation on real hardware: Achieves 85.95% (untargeted) and 81.81% (targeted) success rates across three indoor environments on off-the-shelf hardware, while keeping detection rates below 30%, exposing critical vulnerabilities in current BFI deployments and providing actionable countermeasures.

Method Overview

BFI fundamentals and attack objectives: In a MIMO system with $K$ subcarriers and $M_T$ transmit antennas, beamforming performs singular value decomposition $H_k=U_k\Sigma_k V_k^H$ on the channel matrix, takes the first $N_s$ columns of $V_k$ to form the steering matrix, and quantizes the feedback angles. The attack objective is defined on the classifier $F(\cdot)$: untargeted attacks require $F(x’)\neq F(x)$, targeted attacks require $F(x’)=r_t$, with the adversarial sample $x’=f_{pre}(I(V,s))$.Problem formulation and solution: The behavior time window (duration $T$) is uniformly divided into $L$ segments (with $L=50$ in the paper). For each candidate time instant $t_l$, the binary injection flag $b_l$ and complex perturbation vector $n_l$ are optimized. The forged BFI is constructed as $d_l=\text{IFFT}([n_l,\text{FFT}(d_{base})_{:,2:K}])$ and normalized column-wise. Using logit scores as feedback, the optimization minimizes $\min c_1 L_u(x’)+c_2|b|_1+c_3|\delta|_F$ under sparsity and low-distortion constraints to maximize misclassification; since the injection timing is discrete and non-differentiable, enhanced particle swarm optimization (EPSO) is employed for solving.Robustness enhancement:A delayed BFI injection window mechanism is introduced, updating the logit as a weighted sum across delay units, combined with a multi-sample adaptive mechanism to accommodate delay and signal diversity in real-world injection.

Experimental Results

MirageBeam successfully hijacks BFI-based sensing with high success rates while maintaining low detection rates across three indoor environments: As shown in Fig. 14, its targeted attack success rates (TASR) in the meeting room, office, and hall are 83.04%, 75.46%, and 86.94%, respectively; as shown in Fig. 13, the untargeted attack success rates (UASR) are 83%, 82.9%, and 91.96%, respectively. Compared with the random injection baseline, MirageBeam is significantly superior under the same injection volume—untargeted attacks achieve 62%/90%/97% for W/WB/S, whereas the random baseline achieves only 2.5%/57.5%/57.5%. On existing systems (Fig. 22), untargeted success rates against BeamSense and MuseFi mostly exceed 80%. Under black-box settings, the UASR of a 5-layer CNN and Transformer enhanced via model ensembling reaches 88.30% and 88.94%, respectively. Regarding content-level defenses, the baseline detection rate is as high as 50.56%, while MirageBeam remains below 30%. Overall, the paper reports an untargeted attack success rate of 85.95%, a targeted attack success rate of 81.81%, and a detection rate below 30%.

Channel-Robust RF Fingerprinting Extraction Method for Low-Latency 5G Device Identification in SIMO Scenarios

Yingjie Sun and Guyue Li and Hongfu Chou and Aiqun Hu

2026 IEEE Wireless Communications and Networking Conference (WCNC)

Abstract

Key Contributions

  • Proposes a novel RFF scheme utilizing multi-antenna received signals: Only a single uplink transmission is required to mitigate multipath channel effects, thereby reducing identification latency, and the method can be theoretically proven to be robust against multipath channels.
  • Computes the Log-Linear Delta Ratio (LLDR) between CFRs: Distinct from traditional single-domain signal processing; to address CFR fluctuations, the frequency band is divided and RFF features are extracted within each smooth subband.
  • Simulation validation based on MATLAB 5G NR Toolbox: Simulations are conducted for 30 virtual UEs under different SNRs, and results show that in a TDL 20-tap channel at an SNR of 20 dB, an accuracy of 96.13% and an over-the-air latency of 0.491 ms are achieved, meeting URLLC latency requirements.

Method Overview

System and signal model:In a SIMO system, the frequency-domain received signals at two receive antennas are $Y_1(k)=R_1(k)H_1(k)T(k)X(k)+N_1(k)$, $Y_2(k)=R_2(k)H_2(k)T(k)X(k)+N_2(k)$, where $T(k)$ denotes the transmit impairment, $R_1(k),R_2(k)$ denote the receive impairments, and $H_1(k),H_2(k)$ denote the CFR. The channel terms superimposed in the channel estimation results $Z_1(k)\approx R_1(k)H_1(k)T(k)$, $Z_2(k)\approx R_2(k)H_2(k)T(k)$ hinder the direct acquisition of stable RFF.

LLDR extraction and subband processing:The frequency band is divided into subbands to ensure CFR smoothness, and normalization is performed within each subband using the means $\hat{a}_1,\hat{a}_2$. Defining the relative impairment between antennas as $r=\frac{R_2(k)}{R_1(k)}$, the linear difference $D_{12}(k)=Z_{1,norm}(k)-\frac{1}{r}Z_{2,norm}(k)$ and the logarithmic difference $L_{12}(k)=\ln r+\ln\frac{Z_{1,norm}(k)}{Z_{2,norm}(k)}$ are constructed. Based on the first-order Taylor approximation $\ln x\approx\Delta x$, the ratio of the two yields the RFF estimate $\hat{T}_0(k)=\frac{D_{12}(k)}{L_{12}(k)}\approx T_0(k)$, which is independent of $H_1(k),H_2(k)$, achieving multipath suppression; repeating per subband and concatenating yields the channel-robust RFF, which is then fed into a CNN for identification.

Experimental Results

The proposed scheme significantly outperforms all baseline methods under multipath channels while meeting URLLC latency requirements: As shown in Fig. 7, at 25 dB SNR, the proposed method achieves 96.2% accuracy, outperforming all baselines, with Benchmark1 at 61.83%, Benchmark2 at 62.4%, and DoLoS at 83.67%. Furthermore, Fig. 5 shows that with 4 subcarriers and SNR above 20 dB, an accuracy of 96.43% is achieved; Fig. 6 shows that with 30 UEs and SNR greater than 10 dB, accuracy can reach above 95.4%, and with fewer than 20 UEs, it can reach 98.2%. In terms of latency, the estimates are $T_{UE}=34.64\,\mu s$, $T_{RFFI}=81.04\,\mu s$, combined with $T_{TTI}=0.25\,ms$, $T_f=0.125\,ms$, yielding an overall theoretical latency of $T_{air}\approx 0.491\,ms<1\,ms$, satisfying the URLLC requirements of ITU-R IMT-2020.

CIDO-FANet: Channel-Robust RF Fingerprint Identification for 5G Devices

Longlong Shen and Guyue Li and Jingyuan Han and Tianqi Wu and Aiqun Hu

2026 IEEE Wireless Communications and Networking Conference Workshops (WCNCW)

Abstract

Key Contributions

  • Propose a channel-suppressed RF fingerprint representation, CIDO: Preserves discriminative RFF cues without explicit channel equalization, offering low complexity and strong robustness.
  • Propose FANet: Combines masked frequency-domain self-attention with length-invariant pooling to capture frequency-domain correlations, naturally supporting variable-length 5G NR inputs.
  • Collect data from commercial gNB and commercial 5G NR devices: Collected across diverse wireless environments, extensive experiments show that the proposed method achieves reliable device identification under complex channels, effectively supporting practical identity authentication.

Method Overview

Signal Processing and Normalization: The received baseband signal is modeled as $y(t)=h(\\tau,t)*f(x(t))+n(t)$, where $f(\\cdot)$ aggregates the transmitter hardware impairments. After synchronization, CFO compensation, cyclic prefix removal, and FFT, we obtain $Y[k,l]=H[k,l]U[k,l]+N[k,l]$ (Eq. (2)). On the DMRS resource elements specified in 3GPP TS 38.211, normalizing each RE using the known ideal DMRS yields $CSI(k,l)=H[k,l]F_{TX}(k,l)+\\tilde{N}[k,l]$ (Eq. (3)), which still contains the channel term $H[k,l]$.CIDO (Channel-Independent Differential Operator): Takes the quotient of adjacent DMRS symbol pairs within the same slot to cancel channel coloration, $CIDO_p(k)=\frac{CSI(k,l_{p+1})}{CSI(k,l_p)}$ (Eq. (4), with a sentinel value $c_{\max}$ when the denominator is zero). When symbol times are close, $H[k,l_{p+1}]\approx\gamma_p H[k,l_p]$ (Eq. (6)), so the channel shape largely cancels in the quotient, preserving transmitter-related characteristics; each quotient vector is concatenated in pair order to form the final feature (Eq. (7)).FANet (Frequency-domain Autocorrelation Network): Decouples the complex-valued CIDO into magnitude and phase sequences (Eqs. (8)(9)), using a modified one-dimensional ResNet-18 as the backbone; applies windowed local self-attention to the magnitude stream (Eq. (10), reducing complexity from $O(K^2)$ to $O(K(2r+1))$), and a lightweight 1D stabilizer to the phase stream; then outputs a fixed-dimensional representation independent of the number of retained subcarriers $K$ via masked global average pooling (Eq. (11)).

Experimental Results

CIDO–FANet achieves the highest accuracy across all six test domains and significantly outperforms all baseline methods: As shown in Fig. 6, it reaches 98.12%, 96.25%, and 97.27% on E1_L1, E1_L2, and E1_L3, respectively, and 94.70%, 91.71%, and 81.35% on E2_L1, E2_L2, and E2_M, respectively. On average, it improves over ChInS by approximately 7.7 percentage points (with a maximum improvement of 10.43 on E2_M), over ZF equalization by approximately 12.8 percentage points (with a maximum improvement of 25.19 on E1_L2), and over the raw I/Q baseline by more than 40 percentage points across all test domains (up to 45.51 in the E2_M scenario). Furthermore, ablation experiments (Table I) show that performance degrades when FANet is removed and only CIDO features are used; CIDO–FANet improves over the CIDO baseline by approximately 2.6 percentage points on average, still delivering a +2.83 percentage point gain in the most challenging mobility domain E2_M (81.35% vs. 78.52%).

Secret Key Generation Based on Manipulated Channel Measurement Matching

DU Y, DAI H, LIU H, et al.

IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing

Abstract

Core Contributions

  • Proposes a new key generation paradigm based on manipulated channel measurement matching, supporting key presetting: By applying random permutation or editing manipulation to channel measurements, key information is encoded in the manipulation patterns (permutation order or editing patterns), circumventing the non-reciprocity issues in traditional quantization processes; meanwhile, one party is allowed to preset the key during the channel sampling phase and transmit encrypted messages with data packets, reducing communication overhead.
  • Designs BM-SKG: a high-security algorithm based on sparse bipartite graph matching: Channel segments are modeled as a weighted bipartite graph, and the permutation order is inferred via an edge-pruning improved minimum-weight bipartite graph matching algorithm, reducing the time complexity to O(M2log⁡M)O(M^2 \log M); 64 segments provide equivalent encryption strength exceeding 256 bits, with a key generation time of approximately 27 milliseconds at 128-bit encryption strength, suitable for scenarios with sufficient computational resources and high security requirements.
  • Designs SA-SKG: an efficient algorithm based on edit sequence alignment: Dynamic programming is used to compute edit distance and identify insertion/deletion operation patterns, with a time complexity of ; 64 segments provide equivalent encryption strength exceeding 128 bits, with a generation time of approximately 9 milliseconds at 128-bit strength, suitable for time-sensitive scenarios; combined with the channel multiplexing mechanism, the key generation rate can reach 4 bps.
  • Designs an efficient information reconciliation scheme and channel multiplexing mechanism, validated by NIST randomness tests: A lightweight two-round challenge-response information reconciliation scheme is proposed, correcting occasional mismatches without traditional multi-round interactions; the channel multiplexing mechanism supports multiple rounds of key generation; all 9 NIST randomness tests pass with 99% confidence across four experimental scenarios.

Method Overview

Both algorithms share the same first two stages.Channel sampling stage, where Alice and Bob exchange probing signals and respectively obtain RSS or CSI channel measurement sequences VAV_A and VBV_B.Entropy-based random shuffling and segmentation stage, where Alice shuffles in a random order satisfying entropy requirements VAV_Aand notifies Bob; both parties synchronously shuffle and then divide their sequences into equal-length segments of MM channel segments, enhancing sequence complexity while preserving reciprocity.Segment permutation and matching stage of BM-SKG: Alice applies a random permutation POPO to the segment sequence and makes it public; Bob models it as a weighted bipartite graph and infers via improved minimum-weight matching POPO, using POPO as the key.Segment editing and alignment stage of SA-SKG: Alice randomly inserts/deletes some segments in the segment sequence and makes it public; Bob identifies the editing patterns (insertion positions II and deletion positions JJ) by computing edit distance, using I∥JI \| J as the key. Both algorithms finally undergo information reconciliation and privacy amplification to generate the final key.

Experimental Results

Secure and Anonymous Multi-Channel Update Protocol for Payment Channel Networks

WANG J, GAO S, LI G,

IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security

Abstract

Core Contributions

  • First to propose a UTXO protocol that simultaneously achieves MCU, internal anonymity, and constant collateral: Existing UTXO schemes (e.g., AMCU, Thora) require all participants to share each other's public addresses to collaboratively generate enabling payment transactions, allowing intermediate parties to easily infer the identities of the sender and receiver; HTLC schemes offer anonymity but only support multi-hop single-path payments (MHP) and face the linear collateral problem.   • Proposes EPTT to address the balance security issue in the naive UGS scheme: In the naive UGS scheme, a malicious trigger can delay the publication of txep1 until after T−3Δ−tcT – 3Δ – t_c, causing receivers in other subgraphs to be unable to complete on-chain forced payment due to insufficient time, thereby breaking atomicity. Method Overview

The PCN is modeled as a weighted graph G:=(V,E)G := (V, E), where the node set VV represents user blockchain addresses and the edge set EE represents opened payment channels.The SAMCU protocolis executed in six phases:Initializationphase: the Dealer performs update graph segmentation and generates parameters required for txrelay;Pre-Setup-SG phase: each receiver Ri,jR_{i,j}generates txin and txep1, where txep1 contains only the outputs of receivers within the same subgraph and one special output for cross-subgraph notification;Pre-Setup-UG phase: the key party generates txrelay and txep2 and distributes the relevant parameters to each subgraph;Setup phase: the channel sender generates txstate, txrefund, and txp1/p2;Confirmation phase: a two-step notification mechanism ensures that all channels have completed state updates and all receivers hold valid txep1 before entering the final phase;Finalization phase: off-chain state updates are completed, and if a dispute arises, the protocol enters the Response phase, where each receiver enforces payment on-chain according to the EPTT tree structure. The UGS partitioning principle requires that senders and receivers must serve as key parties, each subgraph must contain at least one segment of each chain, and the update graph must be partitioned into at least three subgraphs to prevent intermediate parties from simultaneously learning the identities of the sender and receiver.

Abstract

Core Contributions

  • Proposes the DCO analog encryption scheme, reducing double quantization loss to a single one to enhance transmission reliability: Unlike traditional digital XOR encryption, which requires CSI quantization, DCO directly drives constellation rotation with analog CSI values, avoiding the secondary accumulation of quantization errors; theoretical derivation proves Pr⁡DCO<Pr⁡XOR\Pr_{DCO} < \Pr_{XOR}, i.e., the SER of DCO is strictly lower than that of the XOR scheme, achieving a 4.5 dB SNR gain at SER = 0.1 under QPSK modulation.
  • Designs a time-varying rotation pattern generation method that jointly exploits channel randomness and message randomness to enhance security in slowly varying environments: The rotation angle is jointly determined by the current CSI and the previously transmitted message of the previous frame, with a chaotic sequence introduced to ensure the unpredictability of the rotation pattern; theoretical analysis proves that the average secrecy capacity of DCO is higher than that of the XOR scheme (CsDCO>CsXORC_s^{DCO} > C_s^{XOR}), and at γˉm=10\bar{\gamma}_m = 10γ, the secrecy capacity gain reaches 0.1 bps, effectively resisting five typical threats including differential attacks, injection attacks, and channel replay attacks.
  • Comprehensively validates the security and reliability of the DCO scheme through theoretical analysis and simulation experiments: Comparisons with the XOR scheme across multiple dimensions, including secrecy capacity, outage probability, SER, and phase error robustness, consistently confirm the superiority of DCO; computational complexity is also analyzed, demonstrating the feasibility of DCO for hardware-constrained physical layer security implementations.

Method Overview

The system adopts a classic three-party TDD-OFDM model (Alice, Bob, Eve), where Alice and Bob alternately transmit pilots to perform channel sampling and obtain highly correlated channel estimates h^A(k)\hat{h}_A(k) and h^B(k)\hat{h}_B(k)。DCO encryption processis divided into two core modules:Constellation rotation pattern generation modulejointly processes the phase component of the current CSI and the previous frame message through a chaotic map (Logistic map) to generate a time-varying rotation angle sequence θ(k)\theta(k), whose randomness originates from both channel randomness and message randomness;In the constellation confusion and clarification module, Alice applies rotational encryption to the modulated symbol x(k)x(k) as xc(k)=x(k)⋅ejθ(k)x_c(k) = x(k) \cdot e^{j\theta(k)}before transmission, and Bob reconstructs the rotation angle using his own CSI and performs inverse rotation x^(k)=xc(k)⋅e−jθ^(k)\hat{x}(k) = x_c(k) \cdot e^{-j\hat{\theta}(k)} for decryption. Since Eve cannot obtain CSI related to Bob and is unaware of the previous frame message, the received constellation points are uniformly distributed across the entire constellation space, preventing the recovery of valid information.
