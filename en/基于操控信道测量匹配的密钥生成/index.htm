Secret Key Generation Based on Manipulated Channel Measurement Matching

DU Y, DAI H, LIU H, et al.

IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing

📌 Abstract

💡 Core Contributions

  • Proposes a new key generation paradigm based on manipulated channel measurement matching, supporting key presetting: By applying random permutation or editing manipulation to channel measurements, key information is encoded in the manipulation patterns (permutation order or editing patterns), circumventing the non-reciprocity issues in traditional quantization processes; meanwhile, one party is allowed to preset the key during the channel sampling phase and transmit encrypted messages with data packets, reducing communication overhead.
  • Designs BM-SKG: a high-security algorithm based on sparse bipartite graph matching: Channel segments are modeled as a weighted bipartite graph, and the permutation order is inferred via an edge-pruning improved minimum-weight bipartite graph matching algorithm, reducing the time complexity to O(M2log⁡M)O(M^2 \log M); 64 segments provide equivalent encryption strength exceeding 256 bits, with a key generation time of approximately 27 milliseconds at 128-bit encryption strength, suitable for scenarios with sufficient computational resources and high security requirements.
  • Designs SA-SKG: an efficient algorithm based on edit sequence alignment: Dynamic programming is used to compute edit distance and identify insertion/deletion operation patterns, with a time complexity of ; 64 segments provide equivalent encryption strength exceeding 128 bits, with a generation time of approximately 9 milliseconds at 128-bit strength, suitable for time-sensitive scenarios; combined with the channel multiplexing mechanism, the key generation rate can reach 4 bps.
  • Designs an efficient information reconciliation scheme and channel multiplexing mechanism, validated by NIST randomness tests: A lightweight two-round challenge-response information reconciliation scheme is proposed, correcting occasional mismatches without traditional multi-round interactions; the channel multiplexing mechanism supports multiple rounds of key generation; all 9 NIST randomness tests pass with 99% confidence across four experimental scenarios.

🔧 Method Overview

Both algorithms share the same first two stages.Channel sampling stage, where Alice and Bob exchange probing signals and respectively obtain RSS or CSI channel measurement sequences VAV_A and VBV_B.Entropy-based random shuffling and segmentation stage, where Alice shuffles in a random order satisfying entropy requirements VAV_Aand notifies Bob; both parties synchronously shuffle and then divide their sequences into equal-length segments of MM channel segments, enhancing sequence complexity while preserving reciprocity.Segment permutation and matching stage of BM-SKG: Alice applies a random permutation POPO to the segment sequence and makes it public; Bob models it as a weighted bipartite graph and infers via improved minimum-weight matching POPO, using POPO as the key.Segment editing and alignment stage of SA-SKG: Alice randomly inserts/deletes some segments in the segment sequence and makes it public; Bob identifies the editing patterns (insertion positions II and deletion positions JJ) by computing edit distance, using I∥JI \| J as the key. Both algorithms finally undergo information reconciliation and privacy amplification to generate the final key.
